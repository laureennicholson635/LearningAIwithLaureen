The five things, one at a time

1. Personal email: a morning list of who is waiting on you

You said: personal emails.
What it does
Every morning, before you open your inbox, you get one short list: who is waiting on a reply, what they asked, and the oldest first. Newsletters and FYIs are skipped. Replies you approve get drafted in your voice, and you press send.
How I'd set it up
Claude connected to your Gmail or Outlook, with a scheduled morning task and a short "how Samantha writes" instruction. One afternoon.
Runs on
Your email account and a Claude Pro subscription in your name. I never hold your mail.
The rule
It drafts. It never sends. You are the only one who presses send.

2. Sales calls: prepared before, followed up after

You said: sales calls.
What it does
Thirty minutes before any call on your calendar, you get a one-page brief: who they are, what their company does, what you last discussed. After the call, you talk through what happened for two minutes and a follow-up email appears in your drafts with the next steps and dates.
How I'd set it up
Your calendar connected to Claude for the brief; your meeting tool's transcript or a voice note for the follow-up. I do not set up AI that makes calls for you. Those laws are a mess and the people on the other end hate it.
Runs on
Your calendar, your Teams or Zoom, your email.

3. Social posts for new business: a week of posts from one voice memo

You said: social media posts for new business.
What it does
Once a week you record a three-minute voice memo about what you are seeing with clients. It becomes five posts in your voice for LinkedIn and wherever else you post, with a hook written for each one. You approve them in one sitting and they go out on a schedule.
How I'd set it up
A voice-to-posts routine in Claude trained on a handful of your past posts, plus a free scheduler (Buffer or Meta's own) so nothing posts without your approval.
Runs on
Your phone's voice memos, your Claude, your social accounts.

4. Business finances: a monthly picture you can read in two minutes

You said: overall organization of business finances.
What it does
Once a month you export your bank and card statements. Every transaction gets categorized, recurring charges get flagged, unpaid invoices get a polite chaser drafted, and you get a one-page summary: what came in, what went out, what is overdue, what you are paying for that you forgot about.
How I'd set it up
A monthly routine in Claude with your category list, plus invoice-chaser templates in your voice. If you use QuickBooks or Wave, it reads their exports. If you use a spreadsheet, it reads that.
Runs on
Your bank exports and your Claude. Your statements never leave your computer and your account.

5. Getting more out of AI: the habits, not the hype

You said: general help on how to use AI to increase productivity.
What it does
You leave with a short personal playbook: the five prompts you will actually use every week, where each one lives, and the one rule that keeps you out of trouble (it drafts, you decide).
How I'd set it up
This is the part we do together on a screen share. You drive, I point.
Why it all runs in your accounts: because then it is yours. If we never speak again after setup, everything keeps working. I would rather you own it than rent it from me.
