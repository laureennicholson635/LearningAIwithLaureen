What changes in Zoho Desk, one piece at a time

1. Every new ticket arrives already sorted

The problem you described: fifty a day and the humans fall behind.
What it does
The moment a ticket is created, it gets a category (order status, inventory, damage or return, rush project, new brand, billing, carrier issue, spam), a priority, and an owner. Urgent things rise to the top. Vendor spam and newsletters get closed without anyone opening them. Your agents start the day on the right ticket instead of reading fifty subject lines to find it.
How I'd set it up
A Zoho Desk workflow rule fires on ticket creation and runs a short function that sends the email text to Claude and writes the category, priority, and owner back onto the ticket. Categories and routing rules come from you, not from me guessing.
Runs on
Your Zoho Desk account and your own Claude API key.

2. A suggested reply waits inside the ticket as a private note

What it does
Same moment, same ticket: a reply drafted in MFABI's voice, specific to what the brand actually wrote, with the one thing the agent needs to verify called out at the top ("confirm the on-hand count before sending"). The agent pastes it, edits it, sends it. On a normal ticket that turns a ten-minute reply into a two-minute review.
How I'd set it up
The voice comes from twenty of your best past replies, which you pick. The draft never goes to the customer by itself. It lives in the private comments where only your team sees it.
The rule
It drafts. Your agent sends. Every time.

3. Instant answers for the questions that repeat

What it does
A share of those fifty are the same three or four questions: did it ship, what is the tracking number, what is my current count, how does onboarding work. When the answer lives in a Zoho field or a report your warehouse system can produce, the draft includes the actual answer. When it does not, the draft says exactly what is being checked and when they will hear back, so nobody writes a vague "we'll look into it."
How I'd set it up
We map your top repeat questions to where the answer lives. Only facts the system can read go in a draft. Nothing is ever invented.

4. A morning queue brief for whoever runs support

What it does
At 8 AM, one short message: how many tickets are waiting, the oldest ones, anything marked urgent, which brands have more than one open ticket, and anything about to miss your response-time target. Two minutes to read, and the lead knows where to put people that day.
How I'd set it up
A scheduled job reads the open-ticket list from Zoho Desk and writes the brief to email or your team chat.

5. A weekly "why do people write in" report

What it does
Every Monday: the top reasons tickets came in last week, by category and by brand, and the one change that would remove the most of them. If a quarter of your volume is "did my order ship," the answer is usually a proactive shipping notification, and that is a fix at the source, not a faster reply.
How I'd set it up
Built from the categories piece 1 already assigns, so it costs nothing extra to run.
Why it all runs in your accounts: because then it is yours. If we never speak again after setup, everything keeps working. I would rather you own it than rent it from me. How the first two weeks go

  1. A 30-minute screen share. You show me a normal day in Zoho Desk, who handles what, and which replies you are proud of. I leave with your categories, your routing, and twenty sample replies.
  2. I set up triage and suggested replies on one queue first, with your admin watching, so nothing is a mystery.
  3. Your agents work normally for a week. Every ticket that arrives already has its category, priority, owner, and a draft. We note what the draft gets wrong and I tune it.
  4. Handoff: a written playbook your admin can follow to change categories, voice, or routing without me. Then the rest of the pieces, if you want them.
