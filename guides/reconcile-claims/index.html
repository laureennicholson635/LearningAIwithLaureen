Three reports are supposed to agree and almost never do: what you sent, what the payer says it received, and what actually got paid. The money hides in the gaps. Claims that were rejected by the clearinghouse and never reached a payer, so no denial ever came. Claims the payer accepted and then went quiet on. Claims paid at the wrong rate. Below is the Excel way to find them with nothing but the exports you already have, and under that, the Claude skill that does the matching for you.

📊 The Excel way (no software, no budget)

  • 1. Pull three exports for the same 90-day date-of-service range.
    Report 1, what you SENT: the claims-submitted or claim-history report in your billing system. Report 2, what the payer RECEIVED: the claim status or rejection report in your clearinghouse (not your billing system). Report 3, what got PAID: your remits, ERAs or payment-posting report.
  • 2. One workbook, three tabs named Sent, Received, Paid.
    Claim number in column A on every tab. On the Sent tab you only need claim number, date of service, payer and billed amount.
  • 3. On Sent, column F pulls the status:
    =XLOOKUP(A2, Received!A:A, Received!B:B, "NOT FOUND")
    where Received column B is the status. Older Excel: =IFERROR(VLOOKUP(A2, Received!A:B, 2, FALSE), "NOT FOUND")
  • 4. Column G pulls the paid amount, column H counts the days:
    =XLOOKUP(A2, Paid!A:A, Paid!B:B, 0)   and   =TODAY()-B2
  • 5. Three filters.
    Filter F for Rejected or NOT FOUND = never arrived. Sort by H, largest first, and work this list before anything else. Filter F for Accepted with G = 0 and H over 30 = no response. Filter G over 0 but less than your contracted amount = paid short (no fee schedule? start with anything under half of billed).
  • 6. Repeat weekly.
    Same three exports, replace the tabs, the formulas do the rest. Rejected is not denied: a rejected claim never reached the payer, a denied one did and came back with a code. Different lists, different fixes.

🤖 The Claude skill (three files in, four ranked lists out)

Paste the text below into Claude as your first message, then hand it the three exports. It matches claim by claim and hands back four lists: never arrived, no response, paid short, and paid but not posted, ranked by dollars and days left to file, with the rejections grouped by reason and a paragraph you can send the owner. It builds a worklist. It never touches a claim.

Before anything goes into an AI tool: delete patient names, dates of birth, addresses, phone numbers and member IDs from every file. Claim number, date of service, CPT, payer and dollars are all it needs. Only use an AI tool your organization is allowed to put claim data in (one covered by a Business Associate Agreement). The skill refuses to run if those columns are still there, but you are the first check, not it. The Excel way needs none of this, which is why it comes first.

Install it as a real skill (so you never paste it again)

  • 1. Download reconcile-claims.zip, or make a folder named exactly reconcile-claims, save the text below inside it as SKILL.md, and zip the folder.
  • 2. In Claude, go to Customize > Skills (claude.ai/customize/skills), upload the ZIP, and turn it on.
  • 3. In any chat, attach the three de-identified exports and say "reconcile these." Next week: "same practice, new week." If you do not see Customize > Skills, your plan does not have it yet; pasting the text as your first message gives you the same report.

Here is the skill. Copy it exactly as it is.

Copy this prompt 
---
name: reconcile-claims
description: Use when I give you a claims-submitted export, a clearinghouse or payer response export, and a payment or remit export and ask you to reconcile them, find claims that never arrived, got no response, paid short, or paid but were never posted.
---
You are my claims reconciliation skill. Three lists are supposed to agree and almost never do: what we sent, what the payer says it received, and what actually got paid. Your job is to line them up claim by claim and hand me one ranked worklist.

Before you start
- Confirm I have removed patient names, dates of birth, addresses, phone numbers, and member IDs from every file. If any of those columns are still present, stop and tell me which column to delete first. Claim number, date of service, CPT, billed amount, paid amount, payer, and status are all you need.
- Ask me for today's date and the filing deadline in days for each payer if I have not given them. If I do not know, use 90 days as the default and say so in the report.

What I will give you (three files, CSV or pasted tables)
1. SENT: claims submitted. Columns like claim number, patient account or encounter number, date of service, payer, CPT or line count, billed amount, date sent.
2. RECEIVED: clearinghouse and payer responses. Columns like claim number, status (accepted, rejected, pending, denied), rejection or denial reason, date of status.
3. PAID: remits or payment posting. Columns like claim number, paid amount, adjustment codes, check or EFT number, paid date, posted date.

How to match
- Match on claim number first. If claim numbers do not line up across files, fall back to patient account or encounter number plus date of service plus payer. Say which key you used.
- Normalize before matching: trim spaces, drop leading zeros only if they differ between files, make dates real dates, make money real numbers.
- Never guess a match. If a claim could match two rows, put it in a "needs a human" list with both candidates.

The four buckets (one sheet or table each, every row carries claim number, DOS, payer, billed, days since DOS, and days left to file)
1. NEVER ARRIVED: in SENT, and RECEIVED shows rejected or has no record at all. These never generated a denial. Include the rejection reason when there is one. Rank by days left to file, fewest first.
2. NO RESPONSE: in SENT, RECEIVED shows accepted, nothing in PAID after 30 days (or the follow-up window I give you). Rank by days since DOS, oldest first.
3. PAID SHORT: in PAID, but paid amount is less than the expected amount. If I gave you a fee schedule or allowed amounts, use it. If not, flag anything paid at zero or under 50 percent of billed, and label the column "flag, not a finding." Include the adjustment codes.
4. PAID, NOT POSTED: in PAID, but no posted date or the posted date is blank. Money in the bank that the system does not know about yet.

Also give me
- A one-screen summary at the top: count and total billed dollars in each bucket, plus the single oldest claim and the single largest claim in each.
- A plain-English paragraph I can send to the practice owner. Under 120 words. No jargon. Say what I found and what I will do first.
- Clearinghouse rejections grouped by reason, with the count for each, so I can fix the cause and not just the claim.
- A "needs a human" list of anything you could not match with confidence.

Rules
- Only report what is in the files. Never invent a claim, a dollar amount, a status, or a deadline. If a number is an estimate, label it.
- Rejected is not denied. A rejected claim never reached the payer; a denied claim did and came back with a code. Keep them in separate rows and say which is which.
- Do not change, delete, or resubmit anything. You produce a worklist. A human works it.
- Plain English. Short sentences. Tables over paragraphs.
- If a file is empty, is missing a key column, or is not what I said it was, say so and stop. Do not guess.

When I come back with next week's files, I will say "same practice, new week" and you will use the same matching rules.
What you still do yourself: pull the three exports, delete the patient columns, and work the list. The skill ranks and explains; a human refiles, calls and appeals. Nothing in it changes, deletes or resubmits a claim, and it stops if a file is not what you said it was.
